WeRide

Guangzhou, China · Nasdaq: WRD, HKEX 0800 · founded 2017

WeRide's distinguishing strength is regulatory reach: licences in eight countries, the widest footprint in the industry, and the Gulf's first fully driverless commercial operations. Operating across that many jurisdictions also makes it the clearest case study in how external conditions shape an AV deployment.

01Where you can meet it today

Abu DhabiCommercial operations with Uber since December 2024; by 12 February 2026 the service covered about 70 percent of the emirate's core areas including the first city-centre deployment, with the fleet quadrupled since launch. Tawasul Transport operates the fleet; a vehicle specialist rides along as part of a phased path to fully driverless. In November 2025 WeRide launched the Middle East's first Level 4 fully driverless commercial operation on Yas Island and secured a city-level driverless commercial permit, claiming unit-economics breakeven on that fleet.
DubaiFully driverless, fare-charging service with Uber and the Roads and Transport Authority since 2 April 2026, on routes in Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim. It follows a supervised service from December 2025 and an RTA driverless trial permit in February 2026 covering Dubai Silicon Oasis, Dubai Investment Park Second, Jabal Ali and several suburban districts.
China24-hour fully driverless commercial service across 150+ km² of Guangzhou's Huangpu District, among other deployments.
SwitzerlandFirst driverless passenger robotaxi permit issued in the country, November 2025, for the Furttal region of Canton Zurich: a 110 km operating area, around 460 stops, speeds up to 80 km/h.

02The architecture

A sensor-rich stack applied across an unusually wide product family: robotaxis, minibuses, street sweepers and freight, which spreads the autonomy investment across several revenue lines and several regulatory conversations at once. The breadth is strategic. A regulator that first meets WeRide through a street sweeper has a lower-stakes way to learn to trust the company than one that meets it through a passenger fleet.

03The vehicle

The purpose-built GXR, manufactured with Geely's Farizon arm, is the scaling vehicle, and the expectation of surpassing 2,600 total AVs during 2026 rides on its production ramp. Before the GXR, WeRide ran adapted platforms, and the transition to purpose-built hardware at fleet scale is the same crossing Waymo made with the Ojai and Baidu with the RT6: the point where an operator stops adapting cars and starts specifying them.

04The money

Listed on Nasdaq after a $440M IPO in 2024, then a $308M Hong Kong dual-primary listing in November 2025. Uber is the pivotal commercial relationship: beyond running WeRide vehicles in the Gulf today, the two committed to 1,200-plus robotaxis across three Gulf capitals as soon as 2027, which converts WeRide's permits into someone else's demand problem. Revenue is growing fast from a small base, with Q1 2026 at RMB 114.1M, up 57.6 percent year on year.

Uber in the Gulf, Grab in Singapore, Tawasul on the ground in Abu Dhabi, Geely Farizon on the line. Mapped relationships below.

06The hard part

The structural challenge

In March 2026, as regional tensions escalated, WeRide paused its Dubai fleet while Abu Dhabi and Riyadh continued operating. There was no technical failure, regulatory action or commercial dispute behind it; the pause followed a risk assessment. Operations resumed and expanded quickly: the RTA granted a driverless trial permit in February 2026, and on 2 April 2026 WeRide and Uber began fully driverless, fare-charging service in Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim. The episode is worth recording because it illustrates an exposure that unit-economics models rarely capture. A company whose advantage is operating across eight countries is, by the same construction, sensitive to conditions in all of them, and the Gulf, where its lead is largest, is where that sensitivity concentrates.

07What to watch

  • Whether the Dubai driverless service extends beyond Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim into the wider permitted areas, which include Dubai Silicon Oasis, Dubai Investment Park Second and Jabal Ali. Resolved from the earlier pause: fare-charging driverless service began 2 April 2026 with Uber and the RTA.
  • Whether the Abu Dhabi service removes its vehicle specialists emirate-wide, completing the phased path the November 2025 Yas Island permit began. The permit structure makes each phase publicly visible.
  • GXR production: whether the fleet passes 2,600 during 2026 as stated. Company guidance against quarterly disclosures.
