May Mobility

Ann Arbor, US · private · founded 2017

May Mobility took a different route from most of its peers: small, well-understood operating domains, transit contracts with cities, and Toyota minivans. The result is 3.5M rides and a genuinely open strategic question about how far that model extends.

02The architecture

Sensor-rich autonomy applied inside deliberately constrained operational design domains: fixed districts, campus loops and transit corridors whose every intersection the system knows cold. The constraint is the product. A bounded domain shrinks the long tail of surprises, which is what lets May remove the driver in Peachtree Corners while better-funded rivals still staff their fleets, and it changes the buyer: a city procuring predictable transit service signs multi-year contracts that open-market ride-hail never sees.

03The vehicle

The Toyota Sienna Autono-MaaS platform, a minivan engineered by Toyota for exactly this integration, carries the fleet. An ECARX platform deal signed to scale the fleet, and membership of the NVIDIA and Uber L4 ecosystem announced in May 2026, point at the growth path: more vehicles, more compute standardisation, and a second demand channel beyond the transit contracts.

04The money

Roughly $383M raised from a strategically tidy register: NTT and Toyota for Japan and vehicles, BMW i Ventures for automotive credibility, and Uber, whose presence alongside the Lyft partnership says something about how the demand platforms hedge. The sums are a tenth of what the open-city operators burn, which is the point: constrained domains are cheap enough to reach revenue without a nine-figure quarterly loss.

06The hard part

What May Mobility has not solved

Nobody knows whether constrained domains generalise. The strategy reaches commercial operation faster and cheaper, and the Atlanta Lyft deployment is the first real test of whether a constrained-ODD operator can absorb open urban demand rather than scheduled corridors. If it can, May has built one of the industry's most capital-efficient paths to scale. If it does not, the business remains a set of well-run local deployments, which is a viable company of a different shape from the open-city operators. The evidence to settle it does not yet exist.

07What to watch

  • Atlanta ride volumes and coverage on Lyft, the open-demand experiment. Expansion of the service area would be the tell.
  • Whether more deployments go driver-out beyond Peachtree Corners. Each removal is a public, checkable event.
  • Whether the ECARX deal produces a visibly larger fleet in 2026 and 2027. Fleet counts appear in deployment announcements.
