■ ONE RIDE, FOUR STAGES, ENDLESSLY REPEATING

A driverless ride is a loop,
and the loop is an industry.

Every driverless ride runs the same circuit: a request, a driver made of software, a vehicle built around it, and a pitlane that turns the car straight back out. Around that loop sit 562 organisations. I have mapped all of them.

The four stages, in order

The Request 53 orgs

A rider taps a button and a marketplace prices the trip, picks a car and dispatches it. The platforms that own that moment own the customer.

Demand & Commercial Platforms53 orgs

Uber alone carries 20 or more AV partnerships, yet autonomous vehicles are still about 0.1 percent of rideshare trips (late 2025).

The Driver 193 orgs

Software perceives, predicts and drives, built on sensors, silicon, data, maps, simulation and connectivity. This is where most of the industry lives.

Autonomy Software57 orgs Sensing & Compute38 orgs Data, Maps & Simulation47 orgs Connectivity55 orgs

Waymo's sixth-generation driver carries 42 percent fewer sensors at a reported unit cost below $20,000, less than half the prior generation.

The Pitlane 70 orgs

Depots charge, clean, inspect and service the fleet, then send it straight back out. How fast that handoff happens decides whether the economics work.

Fleet Operations & Depot70 orgs

Transdev runs Waymo depot operations across Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Austin and Nashville. The depot layer is almost entirely outsourced.

The Vehicle 64 orgs

A production-grade platform engineered around a driver that never gets tired: redundant steering, braking and power, because nobody is left to grab the wheel.

Vehicle Platform & Manufacturing67 orgs

Waymo's vehicle supply chain now runs four deep: Jaguar Land Rover, Zeekr, Hyundai, and a preliminary Toyota alliance for personally owned cars.
