Baidu Apollo Go

Beijing, China · Baidu · Apollo programme since 2017

By cumulative rides, Apollo Go is the largest robotaxi operation on earth, and the most international. It is also the operator that proved a single cloud failure can stop a hundred vehicles at once, which is why Chinese permits froze for three months.

02The architecture

Apollo Go runs the sensor-rich, HD-mapped stack, industrialised. What distinguishes it is less the perception design than the operating point: a purpose-built vehicle cheap enough, and anchor cities dense enough, that several city fleets are described as profitable on a unit basis, with more expected through 2026. Baidu publishes one safety figure worth carrying: airbag deployment roughly once per 12 million kilometres driven.

The Wuhan outage complicated the story in a specific way. Industry insiders attributed the mass stall to a safety self-check mechanism, meaning every vehicle did the individually correct thing simultaneously, and the fleet did the collectively wrong one. That failure class belongs to the cloud layer, and no operator has published a convincing answer to it yet.

03The vehicle

The sixth-generation RT6 is the volume machine: purpose-built for ride-hail with a build cost Baidu has put under $30,000, which is the number that makes the unit economics claims plausible. Switzerland's AmiGo service and the planned London deployment both use the RT6, so the same hardware bet is being exported into type-approval markets with much stricter certification expectations than the domestic ones it was designed for.

04The money

Apollo Go is funded from inside Baidu rather than by external rounds, so the money story is corporate: Baidu carries the robotaxi programme as a strategic bet alongside its AI cloud business, and several city operations reportedly cover their unit costs. The clearest external price signal came from the freeze itself: on the 29 April 2026 suspension news, Baidu fell roughly 3 to 4 percent in Hong Kong, with Pony.ai down 5.5 and WeRide 4.7, which is the market pricing a whole country's permission structure in one session.

05The partners

The international expansion runs through demand partners: Uber in the Gulf and London, Lyft in Europe, PostBus in Switzerland, AutoGo in Abu Dhabi. Mapped relationships below.

06The hard part

What Apollo Go has not solved

On 31 March 2026 more than a hundred robotaxis stopped simultaneously across Wuhan after a cloud-linked failure, stranding passengers for up to two hours on busy roads and elevated ring roads while SOS buttons returned unavailability messages and customer-service calls disconnected. A joint MIIT, Ministry of Transport and Public Security Bureau meeting followed on 14 April; Wuhan operations were suspended; and from 29 April China stopped issuing new AV permits nationwide. The world's biggest robotaxi operator is also the proof that fleet-scale failure is a public-safety event, and its permission to grow now depends on convincing one government that cannot happen twice.

07What to watch

  • Whether the reported 23 July 2026 permit resumption is confirmed officially, and whether Wuhan service restarts under it. Reported by Bloomberg two days before this site's compilation date; treat as reported, not confirmed.
  • Whether the Swiss AmiGo operation converts its special permit into scheduled public transport, which would be Europe's first at scale. FEDRO permit granted 12 June 2026.
  • Whether the London RT6 deployment with Uber files for a UK Automated Passenger Services permit during 2026. Applications opened 22 May 2026.
