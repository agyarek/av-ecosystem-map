Zoox

Foster City, US · Amazon subsidiary since 2020 · founded 2014

Zoox designed its vehicle from a blank sheet rather than adapting an existing car: no steering wheel, no pedals, seats facing each other. One consequence is easy to miss in coverage of the company: it cannot charge a fare anywhere until federal standards written for human-driven vehicles are formally waived for this one.

02The architecture

A comprehensive sensor suite: lidar, radar and cameras on all four corners of a symmetric, bidirectional vehicle with no front in the conventional sense. The perception problem is eased by that geometry, since the vehicle never needs to reverse in the human sense, and the cabin design assumes the driving problem is solved rather than supervised, because there is no position from which a human could supervise it.

03The vehicle

The vehicle is the company. A redesigned production robotaxi was unveiled on 24 and 25 June 2026 ahead of series production, and the 220,000 square foot Hayward, California facility is scaling toward as many as 100 vehicles per week, with a stated ambition of 10,000 robotaxis annually. Building the machine in-house is the purest version of the purpose-built bet on this map: it buys total design control and costs everything a car company costs.

04The money

Amazon acquired Zoox in 2020 for about $1.3B and has not disclosed subsequent funding, which makes Zoox the least legible operator financially: no rounds, no filings, no fare revenue, and an owner wealthy enough that none of that has to change soon. What Amazon buys for its money is a full-stack robotaxi company one exemption away from commercial operation, plus whatever a fleet of autonomous vehicles is eventually worth to a logistics empire.

05The partners

Zoox partners sparingly: Amazon above it, Uber for demand, and a supply chain it mostly keeps quiet. Mapped relationships below.

06The hard part

What Zoox has not solved

Because the vehicle has no steering wheel or pedals, it cannot conform to several Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards, including FMVSS No. 208 on occupant crash protection, so commercial operation needs an NHTSA Part 555 exemption. NHTSA opened public comment on the Zoox petition, covering up to 2,500 vehicles, on 11 March 2026, and it remained under review as of late June. Until it is granted, every ride is free and the Hayward line is building ahead of the revenue it is meant to serve. Zoox has taken the engineering risk and now carries a regulatory one, on a timeline set by a federal docket rather than by the company.

07What to watch

  • The Part 555 decision: grant, denial, or conditions. Everything else about Zoox's next two years keys off this. Docket opened for comment 11 March 2026.
  • Whether the Uber listing in Las Vegas goes live in summer 2026 as committed, putting a no-controls vehicle in front of mainstream demand for the first time. Announced timing is checkable to the season.
  • Hayward output: whether weekly production visibly climbs toward the 100-per-week design rate. The June 2026 production reveal set the marker.
