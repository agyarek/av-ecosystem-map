Nuro

Mountain View, US · private · founded 2016

Nuro began with purpose-built autonomous delivery vehicles and has since refocused on licensing the Nuro Driver to partners. Its largest programme today is the Uber and Lucid robotaxi fleet.

How the driving system works

A sensor-rich Level 4 driver designed from the start to be vehicle-agnostic, which is what made the pivot possible: the same stack that drove a pod with no occupants now drives a luxury SUV with paying ones. The NVIDIA relationship runs deeper than silicon supply, with the Uber programme explicitly built on NVIDIA's architecture, and Nuro positioned as one of the reference drivers in the NVIDIA and Uber L4 ecosystem announced through 2026.

The vehicle it drives

The Lucid Gravity SUV is the launch platform, and the commitment schedule tells the story of how seriously Uber is underwriting it: an initial $300M Lucid investment in July 2025 with 20,000-plus vehicles committed, then a further $200M in April 2026 taking Uber's stake to 11.5 percent and the vehicle commitment to at least 35,000. Nuro builds none of it, which is precisely the point of act three.

Funding

The $203M Series E of 21 August 2025 valued Nuro at $6B, with Uber, NVIDIA, Baillie Gifford, Icehouse Ventures, Kindred Ventures and Pledge Ventures participating. A customer and a compute supplier joining the same round is a meaningful signal for a licensing business, since both have a direct stake in the driver reaching deployment. The valuation sits below Wayve's $8.6B despite a comparable licensing strategy and an earlier stated launch date, a gap worth watching as both companies move from announced programmes to service.

Who it works with

Uber for demand and capital, Lucid for the vehicle, NVIDIA for the architecture, Hertz and Oro Mobility for the depots. Mapped relationships below.

The biggest obstacle

What Nuro has not solved

Nuro's route to scale runs through its partners. Lucid builds the vehicles, Uber supplies demand and fleet capital, and Hertz with Oro Mobility handle depot operations. Nuro owns the driver and contributes to, rather than controls, the rest of the chain. That is the structural inverse of Zoox's position: Zoox depends on a single regulatory decision, while Nuro's timeline reflects three partners' schedules. It is a capital-efficient model whose delivery dates are only as firm as the programmes around it.

What happens next

  • Whether Bay Area service launches in late 2026 as targeted, with fares, on Uber. The engineering fleet is on road; the date is public.
  • The Lucid production ramp against the 35,000-vehicle commitment. Lucid reports deliveries quarterly; the arithmetic is checkable.
  • Whether a second licensee joins beyond the Uber programme, which would prove the platform claim rather than the partnership. None announced as of July 2026.
