SWM

Seoul, KR · private · founded 2005

The operator most Western coverage misses entirely: a twenty-year-old Korean software house that put the country's first driverless taxi on the streets of Gangnam in September 2024, and now carries riders through Seoul's pilot zone with a national Level 4 agenda at its back.

How the driving system works

SWM builds the driver in-house on an NVIDIA DRIVE-based platform, and in January 2026 signed with Lenovo to develop its next-generation robotaxi on DRIVE AGX Thor — the same compute family the global operators are converging on. Two decades of enterprise software work precede the autonomy programme, which shows in what the company emphasises: integration, fleet software and an accident-free operating record rather than research demonstrations.

The vehicle it drives

KG Mobility platforms — the Korando EV first, with the Torres EVX added under a deepened 2026 cooperation as the service spread across Gangnam. A domestic vehicle partner matters in Korea's industrial politics: the robotaxi, the car and the national Level 4 target all stay onshore.

Funding

Privately held, and quiet about it: no disclosed venture register in the Western sense. The programme's economics rest on corporate revenue from the software business, vehicle-partner cooperation with KG Mobility, and Korea's state-backed push to reach Level 4 autonomy by 2027 — a different capital structure from the venture-burn model that funds most of this page, and a blank this map records as a blank.

Who it works with

KG Mobility for the car, NVIDIA for the compute, Lenovo for the next generation. Mapped relationships below.

The biggest obstacle

What SWM has not solved

No fares, one district, and a safety operator still aboard. Everything SWM has proven — thousands of accident-free rides in Gangnam traffic — was proven inside a pilot framework that pays it nothing, and the leap from a free zoned pilot to a 3,000-vehicle paid fleet is the hardest one in this industry. Korea's 2027 Level 4 agenda gives it regulatory tailwind its Western peers would envy; the commercial engine to match it is not yet visible.

What happens next

  • Fares: the moment SWM is permitted to charge, the pilot becomes a business. Seoul's pilot-zone rules are the gate, and they are moving.
  • The Gangnam zone reaching its full 20.4 square kilometres. Zone expansions are announced through the city.
  • Whether the Thor-based next-generation vehicle with Lenovo ships toward the 3,000-vehicle target. A fleet two orders of magnitude larger than today's is a claim to check, not assume.
