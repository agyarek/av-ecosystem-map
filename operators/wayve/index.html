Wayve

London, UK · private · pioneered end-to-end AI for driving in 2017

Wayve sells the driver, never the ride. Its bet is that autonomy scales as a platform automakers and fleets deploy globally, rather than city by city under one operator's brand, and it has raised the UK's largest AI round to prove it.

Where you can ride it today

TodayNowhere commercially, which I treat as a fact rather than a footnote. Wayve test vehicles drive on public roads across the UK and beyond, with trained safety operators.
LondonRobotaxi trials with Uber planned during 2026, the first of a rollout across more than ten cities. Under the partnership Wayve supplies the AI Driver in L4-capable vehicles from participating automakers, and Uber owns and operates the fleet, a materially different commercial shape from Waymo's.
TokyoPilot with Nissan and Uber, announced by MOU on 12 March 2026, targeted for late 2026 using the Nissan LEAF with a safety operator aboard. It is Uber's first AV partnership in Japan.
Your own carConsumer vehicles from 2027, starting at Level 2+ hands-off under driver supervision, with Nissan models first. Stellantis will integrate the AI Driver into STLA AutoDrive, with first North American vehicles planned for 2028.

How the driving system works

Camera-first, mapless, end-to-end: a single learned model from perception to action, deliberately light on the HD-map dependency that anchors the Waymo school to specific, expensively surveyed cities. The zero-shot claim, driving in over 500 cities the model had never seen within a year, is the architecture's argument made visible: generality is the product.

The founder's framing, worth carrying in paraphrase, is that autonomy will not scale through city-by-city robotaxi deployment alone, but through a platform automakers and fleets can deploy globally and improve continuously. That is either the industry's next chapter or a very good reason not to have to run depots. The London trials will start showing which.

The vehicle it drives

Deliberately, none. Wayve integrates into other people's vehicles: Nissan LEAFs in Tokyo, automaker platforms for London, Nissan consumer models from 2027 and Stellantis's STLA AutoDrive from 2028. The asset-light shape means Wayve's fleet capex is roughly zero and its route to market is only as good as its partners' committed timelines.

Funding

The $1.2B Series D closed on 25 February 2026 at an $8.6B post-money valuation, led by existing backers Eclipse, Balderton and SoftBank Vision Fund 2, with Microsoft, NVIDIA and Uber participating and first-time automaker investment from Mercedes-Benz, Nissan and Stellantis. AMD, Arm and Qualcomm Ventures extended it by $60M in April 2026. New investors included Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, Baillie Gifford, British Business Bank, Icehouse Ventures and Schroders Capital. Total capital secured is approximately $1.5B including Uber's milestone commitments, of roughly $2.8B raised lifetime.

Three automakers, three chipmakers, a hyperscaler and the world's largest ride-hail platform on one cap table is the licensing strategy expressed as a shareholder register.

The biggest obstacle

What Wayve has not solved

Wayve has not yet run a commercial driverless service anywhere. Every operator above it on this list has carried a paying, or at least a public, rider with no safety driver; Wayve's driver has not. The 2026 London trials are therefore not a milestone but the milestone: the first test of whether a mapless, end-to-end system can clear a type-approval-minded regulator, run under Uber's operational ownership, and do it in London traffic. Until then the $8.6B valuation prices a demonstrated capability and an undemonstrated deployment.

What happens next

  • Whether London trials begin in 2026 under the UK's Automated Passenger Services permits, and with whom aboard. Applications opened 22 May 2026; Wayve, Uber and Waymo have all publicly committed to participate.
  • Whether the Tokyo pilot starts by late 2026 as the MOU targets. Nissan LEAF platform, safety operator aboard.
  • Whether Nissan's 2027 consumer launch holds, putting Wayve's driver in private hands at L2+ before its robotaxi case is proven. A dated, falsifiable OEM commitment.
