Avride

Austin, US · private · founded 2020

The quiet entrant: a driver with Yandex lineage, spun out under Nebius, that went from partnership announcement to carrying paying Uber riders in Dallas inside eighteen months — while their delivery robots worked university campuses on the same software stack.

How the driving system works

Sensor-rich autonomy — lidar, radar and cameras — descended from Yandex's self-driving programme, one of the oldest continuous AV efforts outside the United States, and now developed from Austin under Nebius ownership. The unusual part is the two-body strategy: the same driving intelligence runs a full-size robotaxi and a cooler-sized sidewalk robot, so every campus delivery mile exercises the perception and prediction stack that carries passengers in Dallas.

The vehicle it drives

Hyundai IONIQ 5s, under a partnership signed in October 2024 — the same factory-friendly platform Waymo and Motional chose, which says something about where the industry's vehicle consensus has landed. The delivery fleet is Avride's own purpose-built robot. Both bodies are all-electric.

Funding

Up to $375M committed by Uber and Nebius, announced October 2025 in the run-up to the Dallas launch, on top of Nebius's parentage. The structure mirrors Uber's wider capital-for-supply thesis: Uber financing the driver that will fill their own marketplace, with Dallas named as the first public launch city. Earlier development was funded inside Yandex and then Nebius, so the disclosed number understates the programme's true cost.

The biggest obstacle

What Avride has not solved

The safety specialist is still in the car. Every economic argument for the Dallas service — normal fares, Uber's demand, a cheap production vehicle — runs through removing that person, and Avride has not yet operated driverless with the public aboard anywhere. Their rivals took years to cross exactly that gap, and some never did. Until it crosses, Avride is a well-funded supervised pilot wearing a commercial service's clothes.

What happens next

  • The safety specialist coming out of the Dallas cars. The single event that changes what this company is.
  • Zone growth beyond the nine square miles, and a second city. Uber has said it wants AVs in at least ten cities' networks by the end of 2026.
  • Whether the IONIQ 5 fleet visibly scales past a few hundred vehicles. Fleet counts surface in Uber's and Avride's own announcements.
