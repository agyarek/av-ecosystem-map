Waymo

Mountain View, US · Alphabet · founded 2009 as the Google Self-Driving Car Project

Waymo is the operator every other operator is measured against: the only company running fully driverless paid rides at scale, and therefore the first to discover, in public, what that actually costs.

How the driving system works

Waymo is the reference implementation of the sensor-rich school: lidar, cameras and radar fused against high-definition maps, validated by tens of billions of simulated miles. The cost of that choice is hardware and mapping expense per vehicle and per city. What it buys is a driver whose safety case regulators have so far accepted at face value, in the form of rider-only permits across eleven metros.

The sixth-generation system, now in public service, is where the cost curve bends: 42 percent fewer sensors than the fifth generation and a reported unit cost below $20,000, less than half its predecessor. Waymo's bet is that scale and generation-on-generation sensor reduction get the hardware bill down faster than camera-first rivals can get their safety case up.

The vehicle it drives

The Jaguar I-PACE remains the workhorse and is being phased out. The purpose-built, Zeekr-manufactured platform publicly named the Ojai began carrying public riders on 28 May 2026 in Los Angeles, Phoenix and San Francisco. Hyundai IONIQ 5s are ramping under a multi-year supply deal with 50,000 units lined up and Arizona production expected late 2026, and Magna co-operates the Mesa plant that integrates the Waymo Driver. A preliminary Toyota alliance points at personally owned vehicles, which would be a different business entirely.

Four vehicle suppliers is not an accident. It is a hedge against tariffs, against single-source manufacturing risk, and against any one automaker deciding it would rather keep the robotaxi economics for itself.

Funding

Waymo closed a $16B round in February 2026 at a $126B valuation, up from $45B in October 2024, with Alphabet joined by Sequoia, DST Global, Dragoneer, Andreessen Horowitz, Silver Lake, Tiger Global, T. The capital is buying vehicles, depots and city launches. Whether it is buying a business is the subject of The Arithmetic.

The biggest obstacle

What Waymo has not solved

Waymo has now filed six software recalls, and its freeway service, launched November 2025, was suspended within seven months after 13 incidents of vehicles entering closed construction zones. The 18 June 2026 recall covering 3,871 vehicles states the remedy was still under development. Meanwhile the company's own target of 1M weekly rides by the end of 2026 requires roughly 7,200 vehicles by an independent FutureSearch estimate, against an observed addition rate of 265 to 300 per month, which lands nearer 6,000 vehicles and 775,000 to 840,000 weekly rides. The gap between the target and the arithmetic is the most honest measure of where Waymo actually is.

What happens next

  • Whether the freeway recall remedy ships and freeway service resumes before the end of 2026. Falsifiable via NHTSA filings; suspended since 19 May 2026.
  • Whether weekly rides reach 1M by 31 December 2026, which the vehicle-addition arithmetic says they will not. Target stated by co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana.
  • Whether London launches under the UK's Automated Passenger Services permits, whose applications opened 22 May 2026, making it Waymo's first market outside US rules. Transport for London consent is the gate.
